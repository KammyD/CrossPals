## The situation

A Canadian seller had a 2,000-unit electronics accessories order finished, packed, and booked for collection. The factory had applied the labels themselves, using the artwork from the seller's first shipment — which had used an older FNSKU and a different carton layout.

Everything was labelled. Everything was wrong.

The seller found out from us, one day before the container was due to be collected, during the FBA label check that runs on every order before packing is signed off.

## Why it would have failed

Two separate problems, both invisible without checking:

- **Unit labels** carried a superseded FNSKU. Scannable, but pointing at the wrong SKU — the shipment would have been received as stock for a product the seller no longer sells.
- **Carton labels** did not match the shipment plan. Amazon receiving works box by box against the plan; a mismatch stops the shipment for manual review, and in practice means it is refused and returned.

A refused 2,000-unit shipment from Canada does not sit in a corner. It comes back — freight both ways, months lost, and a listing out of stock the whole time.

## What we did

We stopped the collection, then worked through the shipment carton by carton:

- Printed new FNSKU labels and applied them over the old ones, flat, avoiding seams and corners
- Verified each label scanned, unit by unit
- Reprinted every carton label to match the shipment plan and applied them over the old
- Corrected the packing list to mirror the physical cartons exactly
- Re-weighed and re-measured each carton against Amazon's per-carton limits

The relabelling took the better part of a day at the factory. The shipment left the following morning.

## The result

2,000 units relabelled. The shipment cleared Amazon receiving on the first attempt, and over $400 in reshipment cost was avoided — before counting the lost weeks.

## The lesson worth keeping

The factory was not careless. They simply applied the only label artwork they had. Nobody had checked the artwork was still current — which is exactly what the label check is for, and exactly the check that gets skipped when a shipment is running late.
