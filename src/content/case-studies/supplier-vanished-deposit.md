## The situation

A UK seller running the same home and garden product on Amazon and Shopify had paid a 30% deposit to a supplier found on a trading platform. Two weeks later the supplier stopped answering. The deposit was gone, the product was never made, and the seller was back to zero — this time with a lesson and no replacement supplier.

They came to us with a specific instruction: they wanted to order the same product again, but they were not going to send money to a stranger again.

## What we did

**Verified the replacement factory properly.** Not a platform profile — documents. Business licence, registered address, tax record, export history, and a live video walkthrough of the workshop while on the call. We checked that the company on the licence was the company that would be paid, and that the workshop making the sample was the workshop that would make the order.

**Split the payment into milestones.** The order was paid in stages, each released only when a defined step was cleared:

1. **Deposit** — released against a signed PI and a confirmed production slot
2. **Mid-production** — released after we confirmed materials were actually in the workshop
3. **Pre-shipment** — released only after our inspection passed
4. **Balance** — released against the shipping documents

The seller's money was never in the supplier's hands without a matching delivered step.

**Inspected before final release.** Same five standards as every order — appearance, dimensions, function, packaging, FBA label check — with the report going to the seller before the last payment left our account.

## The result

100% of the funds were released against cleared milestones. No payment was ever sent ahead of a verified step. Two reorders have since been completed with the same factory, and $0 was lost to the new supplier — because at no point did the seller have to trust the factory on faith alone.

## The part sellers remember

The seller's words afterwards: the difference was not the factory. It was that every payment had something standing behind it — a document, a photo, or an inspection — before it moved.
